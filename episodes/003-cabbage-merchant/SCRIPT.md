## Script

Card images are in `video/public/ep003/cards/`.

### 0:00 · Cold open

**VO:**
> Ever feel like the Cabbage Man from Avatar: The Last Airbender? You watch your Commander plans crash and burn, and you scream "MY CABBAGES!" for the fifth time this week.
>
> Yep. I've been there. Every game. Another plan ruined. Another cabbage gone.
>
> But what if we stopped losing our cabbages… and started weaponizing them?

**VISUAL:** The Cabbage Merchant's card, big and centred at frame 0. "MY CABBAGES!" slams in; cabbages tumble off the stage. On "weaponizing": the cabbages come back with a crosshair.

### 0:15 · Intro

**VO:**
> Hello everyone, this is Wil D. Card with Wild Card Commander. And welcome to the Cabbage Converter: the deck where we take ordinary cabbages and turn them into robots, bears, wolves, even dinosaurs. Basically, it's a machine that turns cabbages into nuclear weapons.
>
> I'm not kidding. I once turned 73 cabbages into bears to kill the table, and my opponent got up and walked over to a wall for, like, five minutes.
>
> And the whole thing comes in around two hundred bucks. About $214 when I checked, and thirty of that is the Cabbage Man himself.
>
> I built it because I was tired of playing normal Magic. I wanted to sit down, say "Okay, I tap twenty cabbages," and watch everyone's faces. So if you want to make a stupid amount of mana and then bury your friends in vegetables, you've found your deck.
>
> I'm going to teach you four things. One, what hand to keep. Two, what you should be doing by turn five. Three, how to win, including three secret win cons. And four, the cards that make the deck sing, plus the upgrades if you want to take it further. One of those secret win cons is so stupid it really should never work. But it does.
>
> By the end, you won't just be a cabbage merchant. You'll be a cabbage master, the person at the table making everyone go, "Wait… how many cabbages?"

**VISUAL:** Wil enters. A cabbage → robot / bear / wolf / dinosaur conveyor (token cards). "73 BEARS" pile; a friend walks to a wall. A "$214" price tag with the Merchant's $30 slice. The four-item checklist, with the fourth marked "??? (should never work)".

### 1:40 · The Cabbage Merchant

**VO:**
> Introducing the Cabbage Merchant. Three mana, mono-green, straight out of the Avatar set. Whenever an opponent casts a noncreature spell, we make a Food. That's a cabbage. And we can tap two cabbages for one mana of any color.
>
> Oh, and cabbages are Foods, so they're artifacts and tokens. Remember that. It matters a lot later.
>
> He does have one little downside. Whenever a creature deals combat damage to us, we have to sacrifice a Food. Don't worry about it. We've got it covered, and I'll show you how later.

**VISUAL:** The Merchant card huge, highlighted lines. Opponent casts a spell → a Food flies over. Two Foods tap → one mana. "ARTIFACT + TOKEN" tag. A small "DOWNSIDE" stamp, parked for later.

### 2:10 · The game plan

**VO:**
> The whole deck runs on one simple truth: the Cabbage Man makes an absurd amount of cabbages. Way more than you think is possible. I'm talking "the store ran out of token cards" levels of cabbage madness. We've had to use dice, poker chips and tokens borrowed from other tables just to keep track.
>
> So here's the plan. We help our opponents cast more spells with group hug, we stack cabbages like it's harvest season, and then we use all that Food and mana to pull off the dumbest plays you've ever seen.
>
> Let's start with the opening hand.

**VISUAL:** Cabbage pile grows past the frame; dice and poker chips stand in. Three-step plan: GROUP HUG → STACK CABBAGES → DUMB PLAYS.

### 2:50 · Opening hands

**VO:**
> Mulligans with this deck are easy, and they're consistent. We only care about two things.
>
> First, and most importantly, we want the Cabbage Merchant out as fast as possible. The sooner he's working, the more cabbages we get. So look for a one-mana dork and two lands: land and dork on turn one, then land and Cabbage Merchant on turn two.
>
> Don't be afraid to go down to five or six cards to find that. I promise the Cabbage Man will take care of you. I once kept a four-card hand: Forest, Forest, Llanowar Elves and a Temple Bell. That's all I had. I still won.
>
> Second, we want some card draw, some group hug, or both. And a lot of our cards do both, like Howling Mine and Temple Bell.

**VISUAL:** A seven-card hand with a checklist. Turn 1 / turn 2 curve. The four-card hand fanned, "I STILL WON" stamp. Howling Mine + Temple Bell.

### 3:40 · Subscribe

**VO:**
> Quick one before the good stuff. If you want more deck breakdowns like this, subscribe. Because this is where it gets silly.

**VISUAL:** Subscribe overlay (about 27% in, right after the four-card-hand payoff).

### 3:50 · By turn five

**VO:**
> So what should a good game look like by turn five?
>
> On turn three, you want to be drawing cards, and even something as simple as Harmonize is a great play. It's four mana, and with a dork-into-Merchant start that's exactly what you've got on turn three. Plus whatever cabbages the table has handed you by then.
>
> And this is where you become everyone's best friend. Remember, the more our opponents cast, the more we benefit. They say, "I need cards." You give them cards. They want mana? You give them mana. And they keep casting spells, and you keep getting cabbages.
>
> By turn five, you want the Cabbage Man out, a pile of cabbages on your side, and the whole table casting spells like it's their job. That's when things get chaotic. People are casting spells. People are casting spells to stop other people's spells. And meanwhile, your cabbage production is completely out of control.
>
> This is usually when someone asks, "Wait, how many Food tokens do you have?" And you get to say something ridiculous like, "37. Wait, no, 41. You just cast two, and they're still on the stack."
>
> Worried about having too much mana and nothing to do with it? Not here. The deck is packed with mana sinks: Retrofitter Foundry, Arch of Orazca, Wren's Run Packmaster, who pumps out wolves, and of course Dark Depths. More on that one later. You'll never end up sitting there turtling.
>
> From there, you're overflowing with Food and mana, and the deck flips the switch from friendly hug dealer to cabbage-fueled war engine. Your Food count stops being reasonable and starts becoming "Hey judge, can we use dice instead?"
>
> True story: I once had to borrow tokens from two separate neighboring pods. The judge started taking pictures.

**VISUAL:** A turn tracker T3 → T5. Harmonize with 4 mana pips. Friends asking for cards/mana. Counter 37 → 41. Mana-sink cards. A judge with a camera flash.

### 5:10 · How to win

**VO:**
> There are no infinite combos in this list, and it's a bracket 2 deck: no Game Changers. It wouldn't be hard to add one. But infinite is boring. You know what's not boring? Attacking someone with a pile of 7/7 dinosaur cabbages. That's art. So we focus on making a ridiculous amount of stuff instead, and it's way more fun.
>
> The all-star is Displaced Dinosaurs. Once it's out, every artifact that enters under our control comes in as a 7/7 Dinosaur. Every cabbage we make from then on? A 7/7 dino. And it's not just Food. We run a lot of cheap artifacts, so your Sol Ring? Dino. Hot Dog Cart? Dino. That random Treasure token? Believe it or not, also a dino.
>
> If the dinosaurs don't stick around, our next-best win con, and sometimes the better one, is Halsin, Emerald Archdruid. For one mana, Halsin turns any token into a 4/4 Bear until end of turn. That might not sound like a lot, but to kill a player from 40, you need ten mana and ten cabbages. For this deck, that's nothing. I've had turns where I made 30 bears and still had mana left over. The bears were just standing there holding cabbages, and it was perfect.
>
> One rules thing. A bear can only attack if that cabbage has been on your side since the start of your turn. Cabbages you made on their turns? Good to go. Same goes for the dinos.
>
> And if you don't draw either of those, it's fine. Idol of Oblivion turns eight mana into a 10/10, Retrofitter Foundry makes 4/4 Constructs all game, and there's more in the list. The full decklist is linked in the description.

**VISUAL:** "BRACKET 2" badge. Displaced Dinosaurs; Sol Ring / Hot Dog Cart / Treasure each stamped "7/7 DINO". Halsin: 10 cabbages → 10 bears → "40". Summoning-sickness rule note. Idol + Foundry.

### 6:50 · Three secret win cons

**VO:**
> Now, remember those three secret win cons? These are the ones that make people go, "Wait. That's how you're attacking me?"
>
> First up is Dark Depths. It takes a while to build up, but once you melt that last ice counter, your opponents' life totals are the next thing melting. Nothing says Magic as Richard Garfield intended quite like removing ice counters with cabbage money. I've had someone laugh so hard at a Dark Depths paid for in cabbages that they conceded out of respect.
>
> Next is Night of the Sweets' Revenge. Most of the time it's a value piece, and we'll get to that in a minute. But look at the bottom of the card. You pay seven mana, which is not a problem for this deck, and sacrifice it, and every creature you control gets plus X plus X, where X is the number of Foods you control. With the number of Foods we make, that ends games fast. I once won with three 1/1 elves that became 52/52s.
>
> And the last one, the one that should never work, is Karn, Living Legacy. Usually we just use his minus one to turn spare mana into digging through our deck. But in those weird games where he lands early and you keep ticking him up, his ultimate lets you tap any artifact to deal one damage. Every cabbage becomes a little nuclear weapon. This has happened exactly once. I had 47 cabbages. The player I was targeting looked at my board, looked at me, and said, "I can't even be mad." And then he scooped.

**VISUAL:** "SECRET #1/#2/#3" files. Dark Depths' ice counters melting → Marit Lage. Night of the Sweets' Revenge: three elves pumped to 52/52. Karn's emblem; 47 cabbages as missiles.

### 8:30 · Crazy value

**VO:**
> If you've made it this far, you're in for a treat, because these are the cards that make the deck sing. Or scream. Depends on your perspective.
>
> Academy Manufactor. Whenever we'd make a Food, we get a Food, a Treasure and a Clue instead. With the Cabbage Man, that's all the time. This card is so good it should be illegal. It's not, I checked. But it should be.
>
> Clock of Omens. This one goes crazy with even a handful of cabbages. Tap two cabbages to untap an artifact. Untap Sol Ring, and every cabbage is basically a colorless mana. Untap Temple Bell or Idol of Oblivion, and that's two cabbages for a card, which is an absolutely absurd rate. I've drawn my entire deck with this, and I've used it with Temple Bell to draw my opponents out of theirs.
>
> Sarinth Steelseeker. Every time an artifact enters, and with the Cabbage Man that's constantly, we look at the top card of our library. If it's a land, it goes to our hand. If not, we can put it in the graveyard.

### 9:40 · Cabbage doubling

**VO:**
> These cards make every cabbage count twice. Second Harvest makes a copy of every token we control: every cabbage, every Treasure, everything. Night of the Sweets' Revenge and Jaheira, Friend of the Forest let each cabbage tap for a mana on its own. Normally it takes two cabbages to make one mana, so that doubles our output. Trust me, this gets out of hand fast. And Peregrin Took gives us an extra Food every time we make tokens.
>
> Put just a couple of these next to the Cabbage Man, say Jaheira and Peregrin, and every noncreature spell your opponents cast gives you two cabbages, each tapping for a mana right away. Someone once asked me, "How much mana do you have?" And I just pointed at the board and said, "Yes."

### 10:25 · Protection

**VO:**
> Now, remember that downside? Here's the fix: Elephant Grass. It's one mana. Black creatures can't attack you at all, and everything else costs two extra mana per creature to swing at you. That cumulative upkeep goes up by one every turn, but this deck makes so much mana you'll barely notice. If your friends start complaining, just stop paying it and it goes away. Or keep paying, and keep making cabbages.

### 10:50 · Upgrades

**VO:**
> All right. You've seen the cabbages, and you're ready to go from farmers market to industrial agriculture. Here are the cards that break the deck wide open.
>
> Unwinding Clock, about $21. Remember how all our cabbages are artifacts? This untaps every one of them during each other player's untap step. In a four-player game, that's four times the mana. Absolutely disgusting.
>
> Fomori Vault, about $15. Discard a card to look at a number of cards equal to the artifacts you control, and take one. With this many cabbages, you're basically picking your favorite card out of your deck.
>
> Inventors' Fair, about $19. Free life every turn, since we'll always have three artifacts. But the real prize is sacrificing it to tutor up any artifact. It's basically a menu for war crimes. Both of those lands can just come in for a couple of Forests.
>
> Collective Voyage, about $8. Great group hug: everyone chips in mana, and everyone gets that many lands. I once convinced a table to pay 23 mana. And guess who had Halsin ready to turn all that mana into bears? The Cabbage Man always wins the arms race.
>
> Horn of Greed, about $17. Everyone draws a card when they play a land. It's perfect group hug, keeping our opponents fueled up and ourselves too.
>
> Doubling Season, Parallel Lives and Primal Vigor. Doubling Season's about $32, Parallel Lives about $40, and Primal Vigor about $15. Primal Vigor doubles everyone's tokens, theirs included, which is very group hug of us. With the Cabbage Man, Academy Manufactor and Peregrin Took out, a single noncreature spell from an opponent can make you a dozen tokens. The judge will make you prove the math. You will. Nobody at the table will be happy about it.
>
> All of these together are about $166, so pick one or two. Unwinding Clock if you want raw power. Collective Voyage if you like your friends. Doubling Season if you hate math and love chaos. Even one of these turns the deck from fun casual into "Why is the cabbage player the archenemy?"

### 12:50 · Outro

**VO:**
> Look, I know this sounds ridiculous, because it is. Magic doesn't always have to be about optimal lines and win rates. Sometimes it's about sitting down, pulling out a pile of cards that makes everyone laugh, and then beating them to death with produce.
>
> This deck has brought me more joy than any tier-one strategy ever could. I've made friends with it, I've confused judges with it, and I've won games I had no business winning.
>
> If you build it, tell me your highest cabbage count in the comments. My record's somewhere past seventy. And tell me what you'd cut to fit in Unwinding Clock.
>
> The next time you're sitting across from someone's $2,000 optimized combo deck, just remember: you could be turning cabbages into nuclear weapons instead.
>
> Thanks for watching, and remember: my cabbages are your cabbages.
