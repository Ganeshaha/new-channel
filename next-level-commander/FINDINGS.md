# Next Level Commander: a Commander channel breaking out right now

Analysis of [@NextLevelCommander](https://www.youtube.com/@NextLevelCommander/videos), using data pulled on 24 Sep 2026.
Visual report with charts and thumbnail sheets (private): https://claude.ai/artifact/JhFF91GVnxWjkB9AfSt2iz

## Summary

Next Level Commander is a 16-month-old Commander (EDH) channel with 7.2k subscribers, hosted by "Jin". For its first ten months it struggled: long build sessions, box openings and hour-long gameplay. On 27 Mar 2026, "This Dumb $50 Deck is DESTROYING My Group" did 134k views, 60× the channel's usual numbers. Since then, 77% of all its long-form views have come from the 30 videos published after that one. Of the three channels studied, this is the closest to where a new channel starts.

Five things made it take off:

1. **One tight format.** It moved from 40–400 minute sessions to 8–10 minute deck techs under a branded series name, "Commander-Centric Deck Tech". Every video ends on the same rating card: explosiveness, resilience, complexity, budget and bracket, in traffic-light colours.
2. **Budget and playgroup stakes.** "$50 deck destroying my group" puts a price and a social stake in one line. Follow-ups reuse the formula: "LOSING to the same deck for 6 years", "The BEST Silverquill deck is $90".
3. **"You're building it wrong."** This is the highest-median format (16k). Examples: "People are building UUGGUU Wrong" (56k) and "The CORRECT Way to Build Azula" (40k).
4. **Being first on new commanders.** Nearly every hit covers a commander from a set that has just been released or spoiled. It's the deck-tech equivalent of Attack on Cardboard's 72-hour rule-change window.
5. **A proven template, plus numbers.** From late 2025, the thumbnails switched to a white-paper, stick-figure, hand-lettered style close to Salubrious Snail's, but built around hard numbers: "0 Rats · 60% Win Rate", "0 Oozes · 70% Win Rate", "Turn 4 · 2 Emblems".

## Packaging

- **Early thumbnails had no consistent look:** dark tabletop photos, card grids, "13 out / 13 in" upgrade graphics, a film-still meme, and a hand holding a box with "$450!!!".
- **The hits share one template:** a white paper background, one card scan, a stick figure or the channel's own drawing of the commander, and 2–3 coloured hand-lettered lines built around a number.
- **Inside the videos**, the hits share a recurring rating card and hand-drawn stick versions of the commanders (for example, Yume). The low performers use a dark tabletop, stock photos and film stills, with no recurring elements.

## Hooks (first 15 seconds)

- **Hits open on a stake, a friend, or a parody:**
  - "My group is being destroyed by this $50 deck and it's so stupid." (134k)
  - "People are playing Azula wrong. Well, kind of. Imagine being my friend Jimmy…" (40k)
  - The Monk Gyatso video (41k) opens on a parody of a show's intro narration, cut short with a joke.
- **Flops open on a general topic:**
  - "The year is 2025 and we're getting a lot of Magic cards." (1.6k)
  - "Mass land destruction has been a contentious topic…" (1.5k)
  - "Magic the Gathering is basically football if you don't think about it." (379)

## What's fragile

- **The look is borrowed.** The white paper, stick figures, card scans and coloured verdict lettering are very close to Salubrious Snail's template. It clearly worked as a shortcut, but it risks reading as a copy. The channel's own drawings of each commander are the distinctive part.
- **Hits depend on the release calendar.** The top 10 hold 58% of views and most are new commanders. July 2026 had no uploads, straight after a run of hits.
- **Long detours cost months.** Thirteen videos of 30+ minutes have a median of 923, and the gameplay series stalled the channel just before its breakout.
- **Meme spin-offs don't carry.** "1 Hour UUGGUU Sounds" did 2k in the same week the UUGGUU deck techs did 56k and 29k.
- **Shorts are barely used.** Eight in total. The "20 Second Deck Tech" series did 5–16k with little effort.
- **Stock photos and film stills** stand in for jokes inside the videos. That's a copyright-claim risk, and they feel generic next to the channel's own drawings.
- **One contrast with the other two channels:** a quick follow-up worked here. "I (also) Built UUGGUU Wrong" did 29k three days after the original, because it was framed as a new "wrong" story rather than an apology.

Next Level Commander shows the fastest route of the three: one short format, a proven packaging system, attached to the release calendar, with stakes in every title (a price, the playgroup, "you're wrong").

## Lessons for a new channel

**Copy:**
- One 8–10 minute format with a branded series name.
- A recurring signature segment, like the rating card.
- A price plus playgroup stakes in the title.
- "People are building X wrong" on new commanders.
- Your own drawn character for each commander.
- A comedy cold open, then straight to the deck.
- Quick follow-ups framed as a new story.

**Skip:**
- Streams, box openings and 30+ minute sessions.
- Stand-alone meme videos.
- Gaps in the release calendar.
- A packaging style that reads as another channel's.
- Stock photos and film stills for jokes.
- General-topic openings.

## Method and limits

- View, like and comment counts are lifetime totals scraped with yt-dlp on 24 Sep 2026. Many of the biggest videos are only weeks old and still growing.
- Formats were assigned by keyword rules and a 30-minute length cut in `analysis/categorize.py`, so a few borderline videos could sit in a neighbouring bucket.
- Captions are YouTube auto-captions (79 of 81 uploads have them), and hook quotes are lightly cleaned.
- There was no access to CTR, retention, traffic sources or subscriber history.
- The in-video frames are YouTube's auto-captures at 25%, 50% and 75% through each video.

## Files

| Path | What it is |

| `analysis/report/` | Published HTML report (`template.html`, plus `data.json` injected into `index.html`) |

To refresh the data, rerun yt-dlp into `analysis/meta/` and `transcripts/`, then run `build_dataset.py`, `categorize.py` and `sheets.py` in that order.
