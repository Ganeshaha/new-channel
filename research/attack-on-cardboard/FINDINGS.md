# Attack on Cardboard: what made the channel explode

Analysis of [@attackoncardboard](https://www.youtube.com/@attackoncardboard/videos), using data pulled on 24 Sep 2026.
Visual report with charts and thumbnail sheets (private): https://claude.ai/artifact/1WxaT8uRL3RGuHwBZjbvdA

## Summary

The channel became the place people go to settle a Magic rules argument. It spent 2022 opening packs for 200–500 views a video. In April–May 2023 it switched to rules explainers, and three videos did 35–68× the channel's usual views. It never went back below that level.

Four things drove the growth:

1. **Lane: rules authority.** Rules explainers have a median of 66k views and rules-change videos 93k. Deckbuilding sits at 7k and unboxings at 392.
2. **Calendar: Wizards' rule changes.** Every official rule change gets a video, and the six biggest did 93k–339k each.
3. **Drama: the neutral judge.** "Can you lie in Magic?" did 590k. The two "Did the Pro Tour winner cheat?" videos did 258k and 244k.
4. **Amplifier: rules-question Shorts.** Shorts have 9.4M views in total, twice the long-form total. Rules-question Shorts have a median of 77k views; other Shorts have a median of 8k. The top Short did 1.08M.

## Packaging: thumbnails and frames

- The same template appears on hits and flops: a cardboard background, heavy white caps and cut-out images. The template doesn't decide performance.
- **The premise does.** Hit thumbnails ask one yes/no question or announce a change with stakes: "Did he cheat?", "15+ year old rule has changed!", "Can you lie in Magic?", with Nerfed/Buffed stickers on cards. Flop thumbnails only name a topic: "Edge of Eternities explained!", "Commander update April 2025", "PAX 2025", "They're back! February update".
- **Inside the videos**, the hits cut to recognisable reaction memes, real players with name captions, and quoted rule numbers (for example CR 509.2). The flops show set logos and card art, with no person and no conflict.
- The channel is faceless, with a box-headed mascot standing in for the host. The mascot is used rarely, and many top thumbnails borrow film stills and celebrity memes instead, which carries a copyright-claim risk.

## Hooks (first 15 seconds, from transcripts)

- **Hits put the stakes in the first sentence.** The mana abilities update (144k) opens with Wizards updating "a 15-year-old rule that players use in every game". The lying video (590k) opens with the question "Can you lie to your opponents... in order to win?".
- **Flops open with overhead:**
  - a sponsor read (Edge of Eternities, 4.6k)
  - "here is everything you need to know… now I want to preface this" (Commander brackets, 11.6k)
  - "it's that time again"
  - "thanks to our friends at Wizards of the Coast" (unboxings, a few hundred views)
- **Pace differs too.** Rules videos run at 145–170 words per minute. Unboxings and early-access videos run at 70–95 wpm, with long silences.
- None of the five videos that open with a sponsor read broke out: they range from 4.6k to 34k. That's a small sample, but it gives no reason to lead with the sponsor.

## Shorts

- The 2022 pack-opening Shorts got a few hundred views each.
- The breakout came with "Wizards broke X" and "RULES QUESTION:" Shorts around Modern Horizons 3 in May–June 2024. The top Short, about Dark Depths, did 1.08M.
- The winning format is a single question on screen, one card, a cardboard background, and the answer inside a minute.

## What's weak

- **Output has collapsed.** Long-form uploads went from 6–11 a quarter in 2024 to 3, 0, 2 and 1 over the last four quarters. Demand is still there: the Aug 2026 mana-abilities video is gaining about 3,300 views a day, the fastest in the channel's history.
- **Streams used up the time.** 65 hours across 25 streams got 11.7k views in total. Q2 2025 had 10 streams and 4 long-form videos.
- **Viewers come for answers, not the host.** Personal videos flop: the PAX vlog did 2.3k and the video about a viewer did 14.6k.
- **Set-mechanics roundups are decaying.** Tarkir did 25k, Aetherdrift 18.7k and Edge of Eternities 4.6k. Everyone covers these on release day.
- **Drama depends on events.** Pro Tour controversies did 258k and 244k, but Worlds 2025 did 16.8k when there was less uproar. These videos can't be scheduled.
- **Follow-ups flop.** "I need to clarify something" did 6.8k after a 122k video. "Wheel of Potential is FIXED" did 8.1k.

## Roadmap for a new channel using this playbook

This assumes a new channel entering the space. Don't copy Attack on Cardboard head-on in MTG rules. Find a game or sub-format where rules are contested and change often, and where nobody owns the "rules judge" role yet.

1. **Weeks 1–2: pick a lane you can own.**
   - Validate the gap by searching YouTube for "[game] rules explained" and "[game] rules change".
   - List 40 questions players get wrong, taken from Reddit, Discord and comment sections.
   - Build one fixed thumbnail template and an original mascot.
   - Map the next 12 months of set releases, rules bulletins, bans and major tournaments.
   - *Gate:* a written lane statement, 40 titled ideas and a working template.
2. **Months 1–3: build the foundation.**
   - Publish one 4–8 minute "players get X wrong" explainer a week.
   - Publish three "RULES QUESTION:" Shorts a week, and cut one Short from every long-form video.
   - Script the first 15 seconds of every video.
   - Make no unboxings, vlogs or streams.
   - *Gate:* at least 12 long-form videos and 36 Shorts, with at least one Short at 10× the median.
3. **Months 3–6: build the news engine.**
   - Publish a "[N]-year-old rule has changed" video within 72 hours of every rules update.
   - Keep a neutral "Did X break the rules?" template ready, and use it only when community uproar is real.
   - For new sets, cover the single most confusing card instead of doing a full mechanics roundup.
   - *Gate:* two rule-change videos shipped within 72 hours, and subscribers growing every week.
4. **Months 6–12: add depth.**
   - Publish one 20–30 minute flagship guide a quarter (Layers-style).
   - Turn comment arguments into videos.
   - Build playlists by problem, and track subscribers gained per 1,000 views.
   - Put sponsor reads after the hook, and check contract and disclosure rules.
   - *Gate:* at least 60% of long-form uploads in the rules formats, at a steady 1 long-form + 3 Shorts a week.

**Stop doing:** unboxings, streams, correction or follow-up videos, set-name-first mechanics roundups, news digests, personal vlogs, and opening on a sponsor read.

**Hook template:**
1. **Stakes:** "[Wizards / the Pro Tour / your playgroup] just [changed / broke / argued about] [rule], and it affects [every game / your deck]."
2. **Claim:** "Most players get this wrong."
3. **Promise:** "By the end you'll know exactly [what to do when X]."
4. **Cut** straight to the card. Any sponsor read comes after this.

**Track weekly:**
- Each video's 7-day views against the median of the last 10 uploads
- Median views for Shorts
- Subscribers gained per 1,000 views
- How long it takes to publish after a rule change
- CTR and average view duration from YouTube Studio

## Method and limits

- View, like and comment counts are lifetime totals scraped with yt-dlp on 24 Sep 2026. Older videos have had longer to accumulate views, which is why breakout "multiples" compare each video with the 10 uploads before it.
- Four of the 233 uploads couldn't be fetched because YouTube started showing a bot check. The same check blocked full video downloads. The in-video frames are YouTube's own auto-captures, taken at 25%, 50% and 75% through each video.
- Formats were assigned by keyword rules on titles in `analysis/categorize.py`, so a few borderline videos could sit in a neighbouring bucket.
- There was no access to CTR, retention, traffic sources or subscriber history, which only the channel owner can see. Confirm the conclusions about formats against your own YouTube Studio data.
