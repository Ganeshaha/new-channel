## The strategy in five lines

1. **YouTube long-form is home.** It's where subscribers come from (0.74 per 100 views on long-form-led MTG channels, against 0.08 on Shorts-led ones), and from 1 Feb 2027 it's the only realistic way into the Partner Program: new applicants need 8,000 watch hours in a year, and Shorts-feed views don't count toward them.
2. **Shorts are for reach and for testing topics, not for subscribers.** MTG short-form reach is mostly on YouTube Shorts: three MTG channels have over 220M Shorts views each, while the biggest MTG TikTok has 225k followers. So make every Short for YouTube first, then post the same clean file to TikTok and Reels.
3. **Every Short points at one long video.** Set the Related video link, add a spoken and on-screen pointer in the last few seconds, and make the long video pay off the Short's promise in its first 5–10 seconds. Expect a trickle, not a flood; that's why several Shorts point at each long video.
4. **Win with named, repeatable formats, not volume.** Every channel studied broke out on a format it could repeat: Attack on Cardboard's "RULES QUESTION:", Next Level Commander's rating card, Salubrious Snail's named concepts. Combo and "infinite" Shorts break out most often in MTG, and that's our lane.
5. **Don't look like an AI content farm.** In this niche that's the biggest risk to the whole channel (see the next section).

## The one big risk: the AI voice in the MTG community

This came out of the MTG-niche research, and it changes a decision we'd treated as settled.

**The problem:**
- **Wizards and Hasbro are publicly anti-AI.** Generative AI is banned from Magic products and marketing. Hasbro's CEO said in Mar 2026: "the audience, the creators, just don't want it."
- **r/magicTCG (1M members) bans AI-generated content** outright (Rule 13).
- **A known MTG creator made a video attacking a faceless text-to-speech channel** ("AI Slop Comes To MTG Content", PleasantKenobi, 38k views). The top comments call for YouTube to crack down on "content farms" and "garbage text-to-speech vids".
- **I found no MTG channel that openly uses an AI voice and has an audience that accepts it.**

**The other side:**
- Studies find people learn just as well from a modern synthetic voice as from a human one.
- YouTube and TikTok don't require a label for a generic, non-cloned voice.
- Our scripts are original, the animation is original, and Wil is a real character. We're nothing like the slop farms that got banned.

**The problem isn't the rules; it's how MTG viewers will take it.** The first "is this AI?" comment on a video can set its tone.

**Options, best first:**
1. **Wil speaks with your voice.** You already record Deck Check, so your voice exists. Either record the narration yourself, or clone *your own* voice. YouTube doesn't require a label for cloning your own voice, and it's still really you. This removes the risk.
2. **Keep Fenrir, but test it.**
   - Put out matched Shorts pairs (the same Short with Fenrir and with your voice) on TikTok and Reels.
   - YouTube announced A/B testing of whole video versions on 23 Sep 2026; use it when it reaches us.
   - Decide by episode 10–12. The later we switch, the more it costs, because the voice *is* Wil.
3. **Keep Fenrir and be open about it.**
   - A specific line in the description, which studies show hurts engagement less than a generic label: "Wil's voice is synthetic. The script, research, games and animation are mine."
   - Put real games and opinions in every episode.
   - Never post to r/magicTCG without asking its moderators first.

**My recommendation is option 1.** It's your call; it's a brand decision.

## What to post

### Formats, ranked by the evidence

We measured 5,659 Shorts from 16 MTG channels, scoring each against its own channel's median:

| Rank | Format | Why | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Combo / infinite / "wins before turn X"** | Produces the most 10× breakouts of any format (11 of 313 Shorts) | TCC commons infinite 13.0M; our ep002 |
| 2 | **RULES QUESTION:** one card, one question, answered | 77k median vs 8k for Attack on Cardboard's other Shorts; the clearest route into rules long-form | "What can you do with Jeweled Lotus now?" 597k |
| 3 | **Ban / bracket / Game Changer takes** | 1.17× median; spikes on announcement days | "This commander CAN'T be bracket 2" 306k |
| 4 | **Power creep:** two cards side by side | 1.4–2.5M on Nitpicking Nerds | "Counterspell Power Creep" 1.4M |
| 5 | **Wil skit / POV / reaction** | Skits have the top median (1.29×), and MaldHound shows personality works in MTG | "How it Feels to Play Mono Red Burn" 201k |
| 6 | **Quiz / interactive** ("guess the commander") | Lots of comments; good for keeping the algorithm fed, weak at sending people to long videos | "What does this card do again?" 355k |
| Avoid | **Unboxings, product reveals, tier lists as filler** | Precon/product 0.72×; tier lists rarely break out | |

In long-form, the proven lanes in this niche are:
- rules explainers and rules changes (Attack on Cardboard: 66–93k medians);
- price plus playgroup stakes ("$50 deck DESTROYING my group" 134k, "$20 deck" 971k);
- "you're building X wrong" (Next Level Commander's best format);
- named concepts (Salubrious Snail).

## How each platform feeds YouTube

| | YouTube Shorts | TikTok | Instagram |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Role** | The main funnel | Extra reach, and testing hooks | Extra reach, and carousels |
| **Link to the long video** | The Related video link, one per Short (set in Studio) | Bio link: needs 1,000 followers on a personal account. Stay personal, because Creator Rewards needs it | Up to 5 bio links, plus Story link stickers on upload days |
| **The CTA** | Spoken and on-screen in the last 5 s: "full breakdown ↓" | "Full breakdown: Wild Card Commander on YouTube"; a sentence in the caption, not a hard sell | The same, plus "send this to your pod's combo player" (sends are Instagram's top signal) |
| **Don't** | Put links in the description or comments (not clickable) | Post videos with other watermarks; use bait CTAs | Post recycled or watermarked Reels, or use more than 5 hashtags |
| **AI label** | Not needed for a generic voice plus animation | Not needed for generic TTS | A grey area ("realistic-sounding audio"): turn it on to be safe |

Instagram and TikTok send only a trickle of people to YouTube. They pay off by growing Wil's recognition and testing which hooks work.

## How often to post

| Weekly | Target |
|---|---|
| Long-form (8–12 min) | 1 |
| YouTube Shorts | 3, mirrored to TikTok and Reels |
| Instagram carousel | 1 |
| YouTube Posts | 2 |
| Comment replies | Personally, in the first week |
| News window | A video within 72 h of any rules, ban or bracket change |

Why this much:
- More uploads go with more views (vidIQ, 10.2M channels), but each video's idea and packaging decide whether it wins.
- Channels posting about 5 Shorts a month had twice the long-form views of channels posting none, while about 35 a month did no better than none (Metricool, 2026). Keep Shorts moderate and on-format, not a flood.
- Expect 40–150 uploads before 1k subscribers unless a video breaks out. Only about 10 % of channels get there with fewer than 10 videos.

## Keeping the channel safe to monetize

YouTube's "inauthentic content" rule (July 2025, clarified July 2026) removed huge AI channels in 2025–26 for one template with the nouns swapped, shock formulas, or daily volume. Our guardrails:

1. Every episode has its own argument or thesis, at least one first-person opinion or real story, and at least one scene built just for it.
2. Card text is never the bulk of a segment. Every rules claim is sourced, with the rule number on screen and in the pinned comment.
3. The Shorts from one episode differ in structure. No daily numbered series that only swaps the card.
4. At most about 5 Shorts a week per platform at first.
5. No AI images, ever. Keep the pronunciation respellings. Never claim the voice is human.
6. Keep script drafts and research notes: they're proof a human made the work.
7. Keep running `check_script.py`, doing the line-by-line read and the "does this make sense and pay off?" pass.

## Metrics and when to change course

**Check at 48 h, 7 days and 28 days.** The full dashboard is in `faceless-playbook.md` §6.

| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| A format at 2× the channel median or better, twice | Name it as a series and give it about 40 % of slots |
| A format under 0.5× the median three times | Drop it |
| Impressions CTR below the channel median after ~1k impressions | Swap the thumbnail, or run Test & Compare |
| Intro (still watching at 30 s) under 60 % | Fix the match between title, thumbnail and first line first |
| Shorts viewed-vs-swiped under 60 %, five times running | Redesign the first frame |
| Long-form traffic from Shorts under ~2 % after 3 linked Shorts | Make each Short's open question sharper; don't post more |
| Subscribers per 1k views | Target 5+; Snail and Next Level Commander run at about 7.5 |

## Money, in order of when it becomes realistic

1. **Card affiliate links from day 1,** in decklist descriptions. Small: 10k views bring roughly $15.
2. **Fan funding** (Super Thanks, memberships, YouTube Shopping affiliate) at 500 subscribers, with a real perk such as voting on the next precon.
3. **Sponsors** (Ultimate Guard, Dragon Shield, Card Kingdom, TCGplayer) at about 5–10k average views. The read goes after the hook, never before it.
4. **Patreon and Wil merch** (sleeves, playmats, tokens, life counters; no Wizards IP) at about 10k subscribers. Kurzgesagt makes 40 % of its revenue from merch.
5. **The WotC Ambassador programme** (creators@wizards.com): early preview cards, a growth lever rather than cash. Apply once the channel is established.
