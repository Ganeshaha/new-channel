# Faceless channel growth playbook (appendix B)

Researched 28 Sep 2026 for Wild Card Commander. It builds on RETENTION.md and the research/youtube-general, shorts, pacing and publishing reports. It also adds figures from our own corpus (`research/*/analysis/*_flat.tsv`, pulled 24–25 Sep 2026).

Tags:
- **[official]:** a platform source.
- **[data]:** a study or dataset.
- **[practitioner]:** a creator or agency claim.
- **[unverified]:** secondary or unconfirmed.

## Bottom line

1. **Channels grow from one or two repeatable, named formats, not from being faceless.** Every case below broke out on a named format, not on volume.
2. **The AI risk is feeling templated.** YouTube's July 2026 clarification and its "AI slop" surveys police how videos feel, not whether AI was used.
3. **Shorts bring reach, not a long-form audience.** A moderate number of Shorts goes with better long-form numbers; a flood doesn't. Which format you use matters much more than how many.
4. **Budget 40–150 uploads to reach 1k subscribers,** unless a video breaks out.
5. **Keep Fenrir for now.** Modern TTS matches a human voice for learning; the risk is how viewers perceive it. Revisit around episode 10–12 with a real A/B test.

## 1. Case studies

| Channel | What and cadence | Growth | Shorts | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Casually Explained (stick figures, deadpan voice) | 110 uploads in 10.7 years | Dec 2015 start → 1.15M views on one video by Feb 2016 → now 4.2M subs [data] | Few | The *idea* sets the ceiling. A slow cadence survives if the channel is distinctive enough. |
| TheOdd1sOut (character animation) | Story videos | 100k (Mar 2016) → 1M (Jul 2016) → 10M (Jan 2019) → 20.8M [data] | – | Recurring characters led to merch, books and a Netflix series. |
| Zack D. Films (3D explainer Shorts) | Many Shorts a week | 2021 → 1M (Aug 2022) → ~26M [unverified] | Shorts-first | One formula. This is the Shorts-only ceiling, and it doesn't feed long-form. |
| Fireship (faceless voice, code) | Branded series | "100 Seconds of Code" was the inflection point; 3.4M [unverified] | 100-second format | A named series *is* the product. |
| Summoning Salt (faceless documentaries) | Slow, 45 min+ | 2017 → 1.25M (2022) → 2M+ (2026) [data] | – | One structure, repeated endlessly. |
| Salubrious Snail (MTG, stick figures) | 4–10 per quarter | ~9k (Jan 2024) → 99.5k (Sep 2026); 7.4 subs per 1k views [data: our corpus] | 9, then stopped | Named concepts. |
| Next Level Commander | 8–10 min deck techs | 7.2k in 16 months; a 60× breakout in Mar 2026 [data] | 8 | A rating card and price stakes; the breakout came from tightening the format. |
| Based Deck Dept. / 3/3 Elk / Really Bad At MTG (mascots) | Opinion essays | 60.9k subs from 74 videos / 43.3k from 106 / 16.2k from 32 [data: our corpus] | Few | Mascot-led MTG channels reach tens of thousands of subs with fewer than 110 videos. |
| The Trinket Mage (mascots over card art) | ~10 long + ~30 Shorts a month | 94.5k; long-form median 41k; **851 Shorts, median 3.8k, only 22 % of views** [data: our corpus] | Very many | Flooding mascot Shorts didn't make them a reach engine. |
| MaldHound (face-cam reactions) | – | 208k; **691 Shorts, median 249k, 85 % of views** [data: our corpus] | Reaction Shorts | Personality and conflict work in MTG Shorts: Wil has to *react*, not just present. |
| Duolingo (Duo the owl) | Near-daily, trend-reactive | TikTok relaunched 2021 → 6.6M → ~15M [practitioner] | Core | A mascot with opinions and running gags works across platforms. |

### Failures
- **Kapwing (Oct 2025):** 278 all-slop channels in the top 15k, with 63B views. About 21 % of the first 500 Shorts shown to a new account were slop. [data]
- **Terminated in Jan 2026:**
  - Cuentos Facinantes (5.95M subs; repetitive AI stories);
  - Imperio de Jesús (5.87M; several AI episodes a day);
  - Super Cat League (4.21M);
  - Three Minute Wisdom had most of its videos removed.
  - The reported total is 16 channels, 35M subs and 4.7B views. [practitioner]
- **Terminated in Dec 2025:** Screen Culture and KH Studio, for fake AI trailers.
- **Common causes:** one template with the nouns swapped, shock formulas, other people's IP, and huge daily volume.
- **Collateral damage:** human-made faceless channels report demonetisation triggered by patterns across about 30 uploads. [unverified]
- **YouTube's 13 Jul 2026 clarification** names three categories [official, via Tubefilter]:
  - "generic or repetitive" content that looks "made with a template";
  - "unsatisfying or off-putting" shock formulas;
  - AI personas posing as human experts on health, legal, finance or politics.

## 2. The first 6–12 months

### How many videos before traction
- **vidIQ (Jun 2025)** studied 5.33M recent channels that reached 1k subs [data]. Only about 10 % got there with fewer than 10 videos, and 68 % had made more than 40. By bucket:
  - 0–5 videos: 5.8 %;
  - 20–39: 13.4 %;
  - 40–69: 14.5 %;
  - 70–149: 22.6 %;
  - 150+: 30.9 %.
  - The counts probably include Shorts, and the data has survivorship bias.
- **vidIQ benchmarks (Jul 2026, 61.2M channels)** [data]:
  - 40.6 % of channels reached 1k subs, 7.9 % reached 10k and 1.3 % reached 100k;
  - median monthly subscriber growth is 1.26 % for channels with 1–99 subs and 0.51 % for 1k–9.9k.

### Cadence
- **vidIQ (Jul 2026, 10.2M channels with 1k+ subs)**, median monthly view growth by uploads per month [data, correlational]:
  - under 1 a month: 0.53 %;
  - 4–7 a month: 1.32 %;
  - 12+ a month: 2.18 %.
- **YouTube:** gaps between uploads are "not correlated" with views per upload. [official]
- **Reading them together:** posting more often gives you more attempts and more practice, but each video's idea and packaging decide whether it wins.
- **Paddy Galloway:**
  - 80 % core content, 20 % experiments;
  - 2× the median is a signal, and 2× twice makes it a format. [practitioner]

### Topics
- A searchable noun works for search demand: rules questions, "how does X work", commander names in release week, precon names.
- Stakes and verdicts work for browse potential: "broken", "WotC messed up", "$50 deck destroys my group".
- The best videos carry both.

### Tools
- **Ask Studio replaces the Inspiration tab** (from Aug 2026), and **Trends becomes "Research"**: top searches, content gaps and breakout videos. [official]
- **Outlier analysis (1of10, vidIQ):** find MTG videos at 5–10× their channel's median from channels under 50k in the last 12 months, and copy the *premise*, not the look. [practitioner]
- **Question mining:** YouTube autocomplete, Reddit and Discord.

### Packaging
- Keep our rules: titles of 30 characters or fewer, thumbnail text under 10 characters, Test & Compare judged on watch time.
- Add one: write 3 titles and 3 thumbnails *before* scripting (from MrBeast's production handbook).
- Expect Test & Compare to come back inconclusive on low-impression videos.

### Shorts and long-form
- **YouTube:** Shorts "will not inherently… negatively impact" a channel, but cross-format viewing is "not common behavior". [official]
- **Metricool (5 Aug 2026; 799,718 videos, 71,177 accounts)**, long-form views per video [data, correlational]:
  - no Shorts: 4,365;
  - about 4.9 Shorts a month: 8,790;
  - about 35 Shorts a month: 4,435, back to baseline.
- **Our corpus agrees:**
  - Attack on Cardboard's RULES QUESTION Shorts did 9.6× its other Shorts;
  - The Trinket Mage's 851 Shorts made only 22 % of its views.

### Partner Program timing
- From 1 Feb 2027, new applicants need 8k watch hours in 365 days or 20M Shorts views in 90 days. [official]
- The fan-funding tier (500 subs) is unchanged.
- A new channel is unlikely to reach 4k hours before 31 Jan 2027, so plan for 8k hours: long-form watch time first.

## 3. The repurposing engine (1 long video → about 8 assets)

| Asset | How many | How to cut it | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| YouTube Shorts | 3 per episode | (a) A promo Short, using our existing template. (b) A standalone "RULES QUESTION:" on one card, answered inside 60 s. (c) A Wil reaction or verdict beat (the MaldHound lesson). Vary the structure; every Short sets the Related video. | Day 0–2, 5–7 and 10–14 |
| TikTok and Reels | The same 3 masters | Re-export clean, with no other platform's watermark. Instagram cuts reach for watermarks and repost-heavy accounts [official]. Say the keyword in the first 3 s, since TikTok search reads speech. | Same day, or the next day |
| Instagram carousel | 1 | 6–8 slides: card → question → "most players say…" → the ruling with the CR number → Wil's verdict → "full breakdown on YouTube". Carousels had a 6.9 % median engagement rate against 3.3 % for Reels (Buffer, 45M+ posts, Mar 2026). [data] | Day 3–4 |
| Community posts | 2 | A quiz or poll 1–2 days before ("Legal or not?"), and a result or image post after. Posts can reach Home and the Shorts feed. [official] | Before and after |
| Pinned comment | 1 | CR citations plus the yes/no question the video argues | At publish |
| Playlists and end screens | – | One playlist per series; the end screen points to the next in the series. Once in the Partner Program, use Shorts series (Sep 2026) for RULES QUESTION. [official] | – |

**"Part 2" mechanics:**
- A Short pays off its promise, then opens a second question that the long video answers.
- A long video ends by naming the next episode.
- Follow-ups work framed as a new story (NLC's "I (also) built UUGGUU wrong" did 29k) and flop framed as corrections (Attack on Cardboard: 6.8k and 8.1k).

**Hook testing:** Instagram Trial Reels are shown to non-followers first [official]; they reportedly need about 1k followers [unverified].

**TikTok Creator Rewards:** 10k followers and videos of 60 s or more [official]. Make 61–75 s TikTok cuts only after reaching it.

## 4. Brand and mascot

**Evidence:**
- Marketing data (IPA / System1): campaigns built on a recurring "fluent device" character are 37 % more likely to grow market share and 30 % more likely to grow profit. [data]
- Creator examples: Kurzgesagt's birds, TheOdd1sOut, Duo, VanossGaming's owl, and in MTG the frog, elk and snail channels.
- A character that gestures toward the content helps learning (g = 0.33, already in our research). [data]

**Making Wil a cross-platform asset:**
1. **A character bible:** three traits (cool, blunt, secretly nerdy), a few fixed catchphrases (the shades-drop "verdict"), and things he never does.
2. **One signature segment per video type,** for example the Wild Card Verdict card or a "Did it break?" stamp.
3. **Running gags that pay off.** Plant them where they first appear and reward returning viewers, for example a recurring nemesis card.
4. **Wil's reactions as stickers and GIFs** for Stories and Community posts.
5. **The same avatar, colours and voice** on every platform.

**Risks:**
- Feeling templated. YouTube allows the "same intro and outro" only if the bulk of each video differs, so every episode needs a bespoke set piece.
- Overusing Wil (see research/pacing).
- Copying Salubrious Snail's look.
- TechSmith 2024: 87 % of viewers prefer a real person to an animated or AI avatar in instructional video. Counter this with human proof points: the owner's own games and stories, and first-person opinions.

## 5. AI voice and "AI slop" perception

**Audience data:**
- Pew (Sep 2025, n = 5,023): 76 % say telling AI from human matters; only 12 % feel they can. [data]
- USC (1M TikTok posts plus experiments) [data]:
  - an AI disclosure lowers engagement, because viewers infer less effort;
  - a specific, creator-written disclosure ("voice is synthetic; script, research and animation are mine") hurts less than a generic label.
- Electronic Markets (2026): AI labels cut engagement most on emotional content, less on informational content. [data]
- Learning (Craig & Schroeder 2017, 2019): modern TTS equals a human voice on learning, perception and cognitive load. [data]
- YouTube is testing "Did this feel like AI slop?" surveys (Mar 2026). [official test]

**Production guardrails:**
1. At least one first-person opinion and one real story per episode.
2. At least one bespoke set piece per episode.
3. Card text is never the bulk of a segment.
4. Every rules claim is sourced (CR numbers on screen and in the pinned comment).
5. The three Shorts per episode differ in structure.
6. No more than about 5 Shorts a week per platform early on.
7. A specific AI note in the description, for example: "Wil's voice is synthetic (Gemini). Research, script and animation by [owner]."
8. Personal comment replies in the first week.
9. The AI-tells check on every script (already in place).

**A human voice later?**
- Comprehension data says it isn't needed; perception data says the effort signal matters. Test it rather than guess:
  - YouTube announced video A/B tests (up to 3 cuts, judged on watch time) on 23 Sep 2026 [official], which could compare Fenrir with a human-narrated cut;
  - meanwhile, post matched TikTok/Reels pairs.
- Decide by episode 10–12. Switching later costs continuity, because Wil's voice *is* the character.
- The owner's own cloned voice is disclosure-exempt on YouTube. [official]
- A cheap middle path is a 10-second human "owner's take" segment.

## 6. Metrics and decision rules

**Weekly dashboard (48 h, 7 d, 28 d):**
- **Long-form:**
  - impressions CTR against the channel's own median (half of channels sit at 2–10 % [official]);
  - Intro (the share still watching at 30 s, 70 % or more);
  - average percentage viewed (45–50 % or more for 8–12 min [unverified]);
  - key-moment dips;
  - new, casual and regular viewers;
  - subs per 1k views (Snail and NLC ≈ 7.5, Attack on Cardboard 2.6);
  - traffic sources (Browse, Suggested, Search, and views arriving from Shorts);
  - end-screen click rate.
- **Shorts:** viewed-vs-swiped (70 % or more good, under 60 % weak), engaged views, average % viewed, subs gained.
- **TikTok:** 2 s and 5 s holds, completion, share of search traffic.
- **Instagram:** sends per reach (Mosseri's top signal for reaching non-followers), carousel saves.

**Decision rules:**

| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| A format at 2× the median or better, twice | Make it a named series; give it about 40 % of slots |
| A format under 0.5× the median, three times | Drop it |
| CTR below median after ~1k impressions / 72 h | Swap the thumbnail, or start Test & Compare |
| Intro under 60 % | Fix the match between title, thumbnail and first line first |
| 5 Shorts in a row under 60 % viewed | Redesign the first frame |
| Traffic from Shorts under ~2 % after 3 linked Shorts | Sharpen the Shorts' second question; don't add volume |
| Returning viewers above 10 % early | Healthy for a new channel [practitioner] |

## 90-day plan (1 Oct to 29 Dec 2026)

**Every week:**
- 1 long video (8–12 min);
- 3 YouTube Shorts, mirrored to TikTok and Reels;
- 1 Instagram carousel;
- 2 Community posts;
- first-week comment replies;
- a "news-window" video within 72 h of any rules or ban update.

**Launch:** one cross-promotion from the owner's Deck Check channel (a Community post and one Short).

**Weeks 1–4: test three formats.**
- **The formats:**
  - (A) "broken new card or combo explained", for Reality Fracture and the Foundations Commander decks (2 Oct) [unverified; confirm with Wizards];
  - (B) RULES QUESTION explainers;
  - (C) "you're building X wrong" / budget stakes.
- **Day-30 checkpoint:**
  - 4 long videos and 12 Shorts are live;
  - rank the formats by 7-day views;
  - check Intro and CTR, and fix packaging first.

**Weeks 5–8: double down.**
- Fill 2 of every 4 slots with the leading format.
- Precon coverage: Mystery Booster Commander (9 Nov) and the Star Trek precons (13 Nov).
- Start a RULES QUESTION Shorts series.
- **Day-60 checkpoint:**
  - does any format sit at 2× the median twice? Name it as a series;
  - are Shorts at 70 % viewed or more?
  - start the voice A/B test;
  - apply for the affiliate programs.

**Weeks 9–13: series and flagship.**
- One 12–15 min flagship on a named concept.
- Prepare previews for Nauctis (5 Feb 2027).
- Build playlists and end-screen chains.
- **Day-90 checkpoint:**
  - output: 12–13 long videos and ~39 Shorts;
  - decide which formats to keep, drop or rename;
  - make the voice call;
  - set the 2027 cadence;
  - project the Partner Program against the 8k-hour rule.

**Healthy day-90 signals (not guarantees):** at least one video at 3× the median, 5+ subs per 1k views, Shorts at 70 % viewed or more, returning viewers above 10 %.
