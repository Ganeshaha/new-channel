# Salubrious Snail: how an EDH essay channel took off

Analysis of [@salubrioussnail](https://www.youtube.com/@salubrioussnail/videos), using data pulled on 24 Sep 2026.
Visual report with charts and thumbnail sheets (private): https://claude.ai/artifact/Awa6TxvcLutaixcGYL5Kn1

## Summary

Salubrious Snail makes illustrated video essays about how to think about Commander (EDH) decks, not reviews of decklists. The channel had about 9k subscribers in January 2024 and has 99.5k now. There was no single pivot. It grew by coining ideas, drawing them with stick figures, and publishing 4–10 videos every quarter for three years.

Five things drove the growth:

1. **Lane: theory, not decklists.** The first video describes itself as "a video about data… data scraping and the sorts of feedback loops" that result. Few Commander channels explain *why* decks work.
2. **Named concepts.** "Smol Bean Syndrome", "counterweight commanders", "the hidden trap of tribal", "the problem with budget cards". A named idea gets repeated in comments and Discord, and each repeat points back to the video.
3. **Craft: hand-drawn and fast.** Original stick figures, a snail mascot and simple diagrams on white. Scripts run at about 205 words per minute with no filler. It's cheap to make, recognisable, and carries no copyright risk.
4. **A face-free personality.** Hooks open on real people: friends Jack and Hans, the playgroup's running jokes, the host's brother Ian. This builds loyalty, and the channel converts viewers to subscribers at 3× Attack on Cardboard's rate.
5. **Steady rhythm.** 4–10 long-form videos every quarter since mid-2023, with no gaps, so the back catalogue keeps being discovered.

## Packaging

- **Thumbnail template:** a white background, one to three card scans, and bold coloured text in the host's voice, with hand-drawn arrows and stick figures.
- **Winning thumbnails** show a two-to-five-word number or verdict: "70% winrate", "only 34 lands??", "Step 1: wincons don't exist", "actual garbage", "Wildly Overrated".
- **Weak thumbnails** use full sentences ("the way to densify your deck without ruining it"), in-jokes that need context, or the host's face. The one face-cam thumbnail is the lowest-viewed video.
- **Recent thumbnails have drifted wordy.** Six of the last eight carry a three-to-five-line sentence. The two recent recoveries (123k, 102k) went back to a short verdict and a simple arrow.
- **Inside the videos**, the hits anchor the drawings in something concrete, like a $ sign over the decklist or "28 lands / 71 nonlands" as bars. The low performers show abstract rectangles, buckets and screenshots of comments.

## Hooks (first 15 seconds)

- **Hits open on a story or a question:**
  - "Last spring a couple friends of mine, Jack and Hans, were having a disagreement…" ($20 deck, 971k)
  - The playgroup's "infamous sentence" about opening hands (manabase video, 348k)
  - "How do you describe a Magic: the Gathering deck?" (tribal trap, 350k)
- **Weak videos open on admin, a favour, or doubts about the topic:**
  - "I'm working on a little project right now, and I need your help" (33k)
  - "I honestly find it a bit tiresome as a topic" (hybrid mana, 49k)
  - "Today I'm going to be looking at a randomly selected deck from one of my patrons" (63k)

## What's weak

- **The median is sliding.** It went from 165k (2024) to 106k (2025) to 87k (2026 so far). Part of that is age, but the recent lows (41k, 33k) fall below every 2023–24 video except the Q&A.
- **Titles have drifted from promises to musings.** Early titles offered a payoff to anyone. Recent ones assume the viewer already cares: "EDH Doesn't Have Archetypes" (41k), "…Giving Your Deck More Sauce" (57k). The concrete "5 Most Common Mistakes" did 297k.
- **Shorts were abandoned.** Nine in total, none since May 2025, even though they have a 62k median and the top one did 248k. The "Is X card draw?" series could run indefinitely.
- **More videos go to fans than to newcomers.** Challenges, patron breakdowns and meta videos (medians of 63k–72k) take slots from guides (177k).
- **The best format is under-used.** Constraint deck stories produced the channel's biggest video, but only a few have been made since 2024.
- **Members-only videos don't bring in new viewers.** Keep the best ideas public.

## Roadmap for a new channel using this playbook

1. **Weeks 1–2: find the theory gap.**
   - Choose a game or format where the discussion is all lists and gameplay.
   - Write down 10 ideas you can name.
   - Build an original drawing kit: a mascot, stick figures, flat colours and card scans.
   - Set up a recurring cast of real playgroup members.
   - *Gate:* a lane statement, 10 named concepts and a finished kit.
2. **Months 1–3: practical essays.**
   - Publish one 10–15 minute essay every 10 days, each with a concrete-promise title.
   - Make every thumbnail a card plus a verdict or number of five words or fewer.
   - Open every video on a story with a named person.
   - Cut one Short from every essay ("Is X card draw?").
   - *Gate:* 8 essays and 8 Shorts, with at least two essays at 2× your median.
3. **Months 3–6: flagships.**
   - Make one constraint deck story a quarter.
   - Start a branded "[Name]'s Guide to…" series.
   - Launch a light Patreon or membership, keeping flagship ideas public.
   - *Gate:* more than 5 subscribers per 1,000 views, and a steady 3 essays + 4 Shorts a month.
4. **Months 6–12: community without drift.**
   - Keep fan-service videos to at most one in five.
   - Frame community arguments so outsiders care.
   - Make follow-ups to your best concepts that stand on their own.
   - Run a monthly title audit ("would a stranger want this promise?").
   - *Gate:* no quarter below 4 uploads, and a median that holds or rises.

**Hook template:**
1. **Story:** "[Named friend] and I were arguing about [card/idea]…"
2. **Tension:** "…it turns out we were both wrong."
3. **Concept:** "I call it [named idea]."
4. **Promise:** "By the end you'll know how to [fix/spot/use] it."

## Method and limits

- View, like and comment counts are lifetime totals scraped with yt-dlp on 24 Sep 2026. The 10 members-only videos couldn't be read, so every figure covers public uploads only.
- Captions are YouTube auto-captions (`transcripts/<video id>.<lang>.vtt`), and hook quotes are lightly cleaned.
- Formats were assigned by keyword rules in `analysis/categorize.py`, so a few borderline videos could sit in a neighbouring bucket.
- There was no access to CTR, retention, traffic sources or subscriber history. The only subscriber checkpoint is the host's own mention of about 15k in the Feb 2024 Q&A.
- The in-video frames are YouTube's auto-captures at 25%, 50% and 75% through each video.
